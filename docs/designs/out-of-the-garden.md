# Design: Out of the Garden

## Problem Statement

The first hands-on playtest (Mac release build, M1 through M6a plus save/load) missed the game's core. In the author's words: "people spread but don't split off and migrate like early humans did", there was nothing to do, and the powers felt empty.

The cause is in the design, not a bug:
- Migration moves surplus people to the nearest roomy tile, so humanity spreads from the Garden like a stain. Peoples split only when cut off or drifting.
- People act toward God only after the player acts: the witness rule interprets powers after the fact. Nobody reaches toward God first, so the world runs itself.
- Powers mostly change numbers. Nothing visibly lands, and a verse is written only if someone happened to witness it.

What worried the author most, in their words: "the anthropologic aspects and seeing the people spread and interact in the game as they changed." The sim already drifts cultures, languages, bodies and faiths; the player just can't see peoples meet, mix and change on the map.

The author's vision, verbatim: "Imagine these are people walking out the walls of Eden and are learning to live with God but don't understand what He wants for them and they do all sorts of things to try to make God happy plus live and stay alive. The line of the snake disregards God's punishment to wander the land and build cities eventually, but the line of Adam finds rest in work to satisfy God."

## What Makes This Cool

God games treat prayer as a transaction: a prayer is a mini-quest paid out in faith, or you answer prayers or lose believers. This game can do what none of them can, because it runs for thousands of years: **replace the transactional loop with time.**
- A prayer is remembered and answered three generations later, and the scripture says so.
- A promise given to a prophet in a vision is kept long after the prophet dies.
- **Two lines record one event two ways.** Dirt writes "the Lord remembered the word He gave". The city of the snake's line carves "built by command of King Vochai". Redemption becomes visible when someone of the snake's line finds the old Dirt verse and recognizes the same event.

## Constraints

- **The design is the spec** (AGENTS.md). This doc is a proposal; nothing is built until it is folded into GAME_DESIGN.md.
- **Determinism (R9-R11):** every new player act (give a vision, answer a prayer) goes through the action log; bands move by a deterministic path with fixed tie-breaks; the save snapshot gains every new field and SAVE_VERSION is bumped.
- **Generated per seed:** the founding moment, and what each line offers, come from the world's seed and the land around the Garden, not a fixed script (author's answer).
- **The Garden, not Eden** *(author's direction)*. The place humanity starts is called **the Garden** everywhere: design, UI text and code identifiers (`eden_tile`, `plant_eden` and the "Eden glow" become Garden). The rename is part of folding this design into GAME_DESIGN.md.
- **Names are the game's own.** Every person, people, place and line name is generated by each people's sound system (`names.rs`), never taken from scripture; examples in this doc ("Ouvra", "Tuvechi", "Kesmiro") are in that style. The two lines are named for what they are (Dirt, the snake's line), not for biblical figures.
- **Faith framing:** God is not quid pro quo. "There is mystery and purpose that comes from a vision that only the Divine can see but some are allowed to glimpse at what is ahead. The prayers of God's people do not return void though." Answers come as gifts (Peter, Timothy), signs and wonders (Moses), dreams and visions (Elijah, Isaiah), and some only long after (the greatest answer came much later).
- **Existing systems stay** (witness rule, doctrine, scripture, language drift, adaptation, settlements, polities, save/load), re-scoped under this opening rather than discarded.
- **Performance:** the tick is ~3.4 ms at year 2000 in Godot (docs/speed-plan.md). Bands and the prayer ledger must keep 100 years/s within the 8 ms budget.

## Premises

Agreed in the session (premise 4 revised after the second opinion):

1. **Two lines open the game.** The first family yields two lines. **Dirt** (Adam's line, named for *adamah*, the ground he came from) starts Chosen. **The line of the snake** does not. The long arc is to **redeem the snake's line or empower Dirt**.
2. **Bands, not a stain.** Early humanity moves as visible bands that walk, bud off and settle (reusing the exodus Band from the M9 design). The snake's line refuses its sentence to wander and builds the first city; Dirt finds rest in working the land.
3. **People reach toward God unprompted** (offerings, prayers, rituals) while trying to survive. **Every prayer is remembered.** Answers are never quid pro quo: they come in God's time, as gifts to people, signs and wonders, or dreams and visions.
4. **You author each promise; part of it unfolds beyond you.** You set the hidden purpose through visions given to prophets. Each vision is a promise you keep in your own time, even generations later, and the keeping is the deepest faith event. Part of every vision also unfolds on its own, so even the player does not hold the whole purpose.
5. **Every power shows on the map,** and people react where it lands (gathering, fleeing, raising an altar).
6. **God may speak through dreams and visions to a few.** This overrides Open Question 2's "acts and signs only" recommendation in GAME_DESIGN.md.
7. **The snake's line resists God by its own nature** *(author's revision)*. Left to itself, the snake's line hardens: it trusts its own works, raises rulers and idols in God's place, takes by force, and oppresses the weak. In the author's words, it should "naturally" "resist God and become wicked, as they were historically." This is its default course, emerging from its starting beliefs and drives, not a script and not the player's doing. Redemption runs against that grain, which is what makes it mean something.
8. **Peoples are watched as they spread, meet and change** *(author's revision)*. The anthropology is the heart of the game: bands meeting on the map, trading, marrying in, quarrelling, raiding, borrowing words and gods, and their looks, dress, houses and speech drifting apart over generations, all visible, not only in numbers.

## Recommended Approach

**Out of the Garden**, a new milestone inserted before M6b (covenants), built in order so each step is playable on its own:

1. **The generated founding.** From the seed and the Garden's surroundings, generate the first family's two lines and the founding event (what each line offers from the land it works, and how they part). Written as the opening verses of both scriptures. Dirt starts Chosen. Every people carries a **line** (Dirt or the snake's line), set here, inherited unchanged at each split, kept by the larger side when peoples blend, and saved in the snapshot *(eng review D4)*.
2. **Bands.** Pull the exodus `Band` forward from the M9 design: a group with a people, a line, a leader and a position, moving by a deterministic path. Early humanity moves as bands instead of tile diffusion; a band buds a daughter when it grows past a size or after a quarrel; a band settles where land suits its line. **The snake's line** stops wandering early and founds the first settlement, which grows into the first city. Its starting belief leans toward self-reliance and away from God (the existing belief axes), set so it hardens on about half of seeds (a 50/50 lean, *author's choice*, tuned from playtests), and its culture weights favor walls, rulers and taking by force; from there, its drift toward wickedness emerges through the existing systems (doctrine forming from what it sees and does, government, inequality, unrest, and later raids and war). Wickedness shows as acts the world records: idols and rulers worshipped, raids on neighbors, the weak oppressed. **Dirt** walks slowly and settles on arable land. **Bands and settled spread** *(eng review D7)*: a band of 8 or more becomes a settled people the moment it founds a settlement (a tier-0 camp), and only on unclaimed land. From then on that people spreads by the existing tile diffusion, but only within the catchments its own settlements claim, never into another people's. Outside claimed land, people move only as bands, and the old density founding outside claimed land is removed. A band plans its route once, over the land's real crossing cost, so bands follow coasts, rivers and grassland and skirt deserts and ranges; it may wade coastal water (ocean touching land) at a high cost, so bands cross narrow straits to near islands and continents but never open ocean *(D22)*; it walks one step a year; at most 64 bands walk at once, crowded settlements waiting their turn; a band that finds no reachable unclaimed land within 100 years of forming heads to the nearest settlement of its own people or line and joins it, or, if none is reachable, joins the people whose land it stands on through the merge rule; a settlement below one person for two checks releases its catchment, so land can reopen *(eng review D10, D16)*. A band carries its own headcount: its people's population, piety weighting and every aggregate count its walkers, and its current tile shows its people on the map without holding tile population *(eng review D8)*. A band that comes within reach of a different people meets it as an encounter (step 2b), weighted by both sides' needs, beliefs and lines: kin of the same line lean toward joining, enemies and a hardened snake's line toward raiding. A crowded settlement buds a new band, so band budding continues all game; it slows as open land runs out. The first era is simply when bands are most of the world.
2b. **Peoples meeting, made visible.** When bands or settled peoples come into contact, the encounter is an event on the map and in the log, chosen from their needs, beliefs and the snake line's nature: they **trade**, **marry in** (the existing culture blending, now shown as a joined band or a mixed settlement), **quarrel and part**, or **raid**. Contact also carries words and gods between them (language borrowing and the existing scripture copies). Drift is shown, not just computed: dress, house styles, faces (adaptation) and speech change visibly across generations, and the culture and language overlays show where peoples meet and blend. The event log tells the encounter in the peoples' own words. **Skirmishes and diplomacy** *(eng review D11)*: a raid resolves by numbers, strength being walkers times martial weight; the loser loses up to 15% and never falls below 8, then flees on a new route. A settled people that loses a raid takes its losses across the raided catchment, never below 8, cut by its Defense buildings; a heavy loss buds a refugee band that walks away *(eng review D17)*. Every pair of peoples keeps a standing, from kin through friendly and wary to feud, that each meeting nudges and that lasts across generations. From Stage 1, peoples (bands and settled alike) can also hold **truces**, **alliances** and **tribute** with one another. When polities form in M6, a polity's relations start from its member peoples' standings and agreements (Open Question 11). **Intercession at a meeting** *(eng review D12)*: because routes are planned, the game sees two peoples converging a few years ahead and marks it on the map and in the log. Powers the player uses in that window shape the meeting: a blessing strengthens the weaker side, a storm blocks a path, a sign can turn a raid into parting. In Stage 2 the threatened people also cry out, opening a prayer the player may answer in God's time or not at all; it is never a trade and never a sure fix. **Within a line and between lines** *(author's direction and eng review D13)*: diplomacy inside a line differs from diplomacy between lines. Kin of one line start friendly and their quarrels end in parting, not raids; peoples of different lines start wary, and raids and tribute are possible. The player can **unify** bands and peoples of one line (a new player act, queued and logged like every act) into a **league of kin**: they stay different tribes and tongues, each drifting in dress, speech and custom, but pool strength in skirmishes and defend one another against other lines. The lines bond differently. In the author's words, Dirt's peoples are "united by their differences still, bonded by faith": a Dirt league holds by shared faith, not likeness. The snake's line "is bonded by strength in traits that only their kind share": its leagues hold only among peoples alike in traits, tribe and tongue, it bonds poorly with anyone different, and it "would rather conquer or overpower", preferring fortifications and citadels in its cities to spreading through the world (fewer bands budded, more Defense buildings).

3. **Offerings and the prayer ledger.** Bands and settlements act toward God unprompted, choosing acts from their need, doctrine and the land (first grain, a lamb, a stone raised, a dance for rain). Each act becomes a prayer record that is never deleted: who, where, when, what for.
   - **Bounded:** prayers of the same kind, by the same people, at the same place merge into one record with a count and its first and last year. Each people holds at most 32 open records; when full, the oldest open record is closed into a compact remembered entry (kept in the snapshot like legends, outside the hot tick path) that an answer can still cite.
   - **Shown:** the HUD shows the open records of the Chosen and of any people with a living witness, newest and longest-waiting first; scripture records each prayer when it is first made.
4. **Answers in God's time.** The player answers any open or remembered prayer, at any time, in one of three forms: **a gift** to a person (a new notable trait or skill, a child), **a sign or wonder** (a power aimed at the place), or **a dream or vision** (step 5). An answer generations later still cites the prayer ("the Lord remembered the prayer of Ouvra").
   - **The act:** `answer_prayer(prayer_id, form, target)`, queued and logged like every player act (D9, R10). A sign-or-wonder answer applies its power at the prayer's place at the next tick.
   - **Once answered:** an answered record closes and becomes a remembered entry, which frees one of the people's 32 open slots. A closed record cannot be answered again. A new prayer of the same kind at the same place after the answer opens a new record.
   - **With the witness rule:** the power is still witnessed as usual, but witnesses of the praying people read it as the answer to their prayer: their belief update and verse cite the prayer. A power cast without `answer_prayer` is an ordinary witnessed event and answers nothing, even if it lands near people who prayed.
5. **Visions as promises.** `give_vision(notable, promise)`, queued and logged like every player act.
   - **What a promise is:** a structured entry, not free text. The player picks a kind and a target from a list the game generates for that world and moment: *a people will settle at a place*, *a place will hold a city*, *a city will fall*, *a people will reach a size*, *a line will turn toward God*, *a gift will come to the prophet's people*. The game renders it into the vision's words in the prophet's own language.
   - **When it is kept:** at each 25-year check, the game tests every open promise against the world. A promise is kept the first time its condition is true at a check, however it came true: by the player's own acts or by the world's course. That is how "part unfolds beyond you" works: the player chooses the promise, but does not hold all of how or when it comes about. The condition each kind tests:
     - *a people will settle at a place:* a settlement of that people, or of a people split from it, stands within 2 tiles of the place;
     - *a place will hold a city:* a settlement within 2 tiles of the place is at tier 3;
     - *a city will fall:* the named settlement is at tier 0 or no longer exists;
     - *a people will reach a size:* that people's population, with every people split from it since the vision, is at least the promised size at the check (tested only at the check, not as a running maximum);
     - *a line will turn toward God:* the line's population-weighted piety is at least 0.7 at the check;
     - *a gift will come to the prophet's people:* the player answers a prayer of that people, or of a people split from it, with a gift.
   - **The hidden clause:** some kinds carry a second clause the game chooses and does not show the player. The promise is kept when its visible condition is met; the hidden clause, if it ever comes true, is a separate later keeping with its own verse ("and more than was spoken").
   - **Only open promises are offered:** the list the game generates never includes a condition that is already true when the vision is given.
   - **No penalty for waiting:** an open promise never wears trust down; God's time is not the player's. Keeping one is a great event; scripture marks the years between the vision and the keeping.
   - **If the prophet's people die out:** the promise stays open. It is remembered by whichever peoples still hold the vision verse (copied at splits, and through contact by the rule below).
   - **Who hears a vision:** the prophet's own people always. Another people, including the other line, hears it in three ways only: its tiles touch the prophet's people's when the vision is given; it inherits the verse at a split; or through contact: at each 25-year check, a people within the contact radius (14 tiles) of a people holding a vision verse it lacks copies that verse, respelled in its own language as split copies are *(eng review D3)*. A people that never hears it has no record of it.
6. **Visible powers, and the plagues.** Every power gets an on-map effect (meteor strike and crater, tornado path, blessing glow, rain) and nearby people react visibly; the verse arrives with it. The signs and wonders expand to include the ten plagues of Egypt *(author's revision)*, each a sign aimed at a people or place, each visible where it lands:
   1. **water turned to blood** (rivers and lakes run red; water-reliant growth stops for a season);
   2. **frogs** (swarming from the water over the land);
   3. **gnats** (a haze over settlements; work slows);
   4. **flies** (swarms over a region; the sign can spare the faithful's tiles);
   5. **pestilence on livestock** (herding capacity falls);
   6. **boils** (sickness on people; labor falls);
   7. **hail** (fire and ice on fields and buildings);
   8. **locusts** (a moving cloud that strips crops);
   9. **darkness** (the land goes dark; the night pass over a region for days);
   10. **death of the firstborn** (the gravest judgment, *settled by the author*): available only against a people that has **resisted earlier signs** (signs aimed at it after which its belief did not turn toward God; the count is a tuning value, starting at three). It takes **10% of that people's youngest generation**. The sim has no ages, so the youngest generation is approximated as the people's growth over the last 20 years.
   Like every answer, a plague is never a transaction: the people read it through the witness rule, and a plague aimed at an oppressor reads as judgment to the oppressed and as wrath to the oppressor. The plagues fold in the Plague and Darkness signs the existing exodus design (M9) already plans, as signs against a captor.
7. **Two records, one event.** When a promise is kept, every people that holds the vision records the keeping in its own scripture, in its own way: Dirt in its scripture's voice ("He remembered"); the snake's line in the voice of a city's chronicle, crediting its rulers ("by the king's command"). The two voices are verse styles chosen by the line's doctrine and government, built on the existing scripture system, not a separate inscription system. A people that witnesses the event without having heard the vision records it as an ordinary event. A side-by-side view shows every people's record of one kept promise.
8. **Redemption.** When a scripture copy carrying a kept promise's Dirt verse reaches a people of the snake's line that recorded the same keeping as its rulers' work, one of its notables may **recognize** the two as the same event. Recognition is the snake's line's turning point: belief shifts toward God, and favor can follow at the player's choice. The trigger and the size of the shift are Open Question 8. Verses found in ruins wait for the existing Ruins design; this milestone uses contact only.

Folding into the existing design: the witness rule still interprets powers; doctrine still forms from what people saw; language drift still shapes each scripture; settlements, polities and save/load carry on. M9's exodus reuses the Band built here.

## Open Questions

1. **The hidden clause.** Step 5 settles how part of a vision unfolds beyond the player: the world's own course can keep a promise, and some kinds carry a hidden second clause. Still open: which kinds carry a hidden clause, how the game chooses it, and how often. Decide before step 5.
2. **The generated founding** *(settled 2026-10-28, D18 and D19)*. The Garden stays on the world's best land. Of its six neighbors, Dirt takes the most arable (highest carrying capacity) and offers its harvest; the snake's line takes the most defensible (highest elevation, falling back to the next-best land on a flat seed), offers the work of its hands, and heads for high ground to build. Ties break on the lower tile index; no RNG. The player watches: the game opens paused at year 0 on both offerings and their verses, and play sends both lines out. No player act at year 0.
3. **What "redeem the snake's line" ends in.** Does a redeemed line become Chosen beside Dirt, merge with Dirt, or keep its own path with a new faith? How "empower Dirt" is measured.
4. **The greatest answer.** The author named the later coming of Jesus as an answer "for freedom from sin and death for believers". Whether and how the game reflects a great late answer is the author's call, and needs care.
5. **Pacing.** How often people pray, how many prayers wait at once, and what happens at 100× speed so the ledger never floods.
6. **Budding rules** *(settled 2026-10-28, D20)*. A crowded settlement buds a band of 25; a band over 50 splits in two; once every 25 years a band rolls for a quarrel, likelier with size and low cohesion, and a quarrel ends in parting. A band that crossed a strait (3+ hexes of water) or a range (4+ hexes of mountain) to reach its land, or whose culture has drifted past the split threshold, founds a daughter people with a parent id; otherwise its camp stays its people's, however far it walked *(amended 2026-10-28, D21: distance alone made nearly every frontier camp a near-identical people; D24: terrain parts peoples)*. Thresholds are tuned in the sweep.
7. **Interaction with existing milestones** *(settled by eng review D14 and D20)*. M1's "3+ distinct peoples by year 2,000" gate stays; new peoples come from bands settling far from kin or drifting apart (D20). Mechanism tests are rewritten under the regression contract (D14).

8. **Recognition.** What makes a notable of the snake's line recognize a kept promise in another people's verse (the copy alone, a belief or piety threshold, a deterministic roll), and how far belief moves when it does. Blocks step 8.

9. **The gravest plague** *(settled: available after a people resists earlier signs; takes 10% of its youngest generation; see step 6)*. Still open: how it is depicted with care.
10. **How far the snake's line leans** *(settled: a 50/50 starting lean, so it hardens on about half of seeds; tune from playtests)*.

11. **Diplomacy between peoples** *(eng review D11)*. The rules for truces, alliances and tribute before polities exist: what makes each form and end, what each changes (raid odds, joint flight, a share of food paid each check), and how M6 polity diplomacy takes them over. It also sets how a league's bond is measured (shared faith for Dirt, shared traits, tribe and tongue for the snake's line) and how a league frays or breaks, and the raid loss that buds a refugee band. It also sets what each power does to a meeting during the warning window (D12). Decide before step 2b is built.

## Success Criteria

- In the first minute of a new world, two bands visibly leave the Garden, and the founding verses name what happened.
- Within the first 30 minutes, the player has answered at least one prayer in each form, given at least one vision, and seen people react on the map to every power used.
- At 100× speed, a promise kept generations after its prophet died produces a verse that cites the original vision, in both lines' records, and the side-by-side view shows both.
- Replay is exact: the same seed and action log reproduce every band path, prayer, vision and verse (R10 tests extended).
- Left alone for 2,000 years, the snake's line hardens on about half of seeds (the author's 50/50 starting lean): its doctrine turns from God and the log records idols, raids or oppression. On the other half it does not, which keeps redemption meaningful. Tuned from playtests.
- Watching any 10 minutes at 25× shows peoples meeting (trade, marriage, quarrel or raid) and their dress, houses or speech visibly drifting.
- The tick stays within the 8 ms frame budget at year 2,000 with bands and the ledger running.
- The design's playtest gate holds: a friend plays for 30 minutes and tells a story about "their people" without being prompted.

### R2-1 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Persisting part of R1-2. The promise model is now structured, but the keeping rule is still incomplete: (a) for kinds with a hidden second clause, nothing says whether the promise is kept when the visible condition is true, only when both are, or partly kept when one is (the partial-keeping rule R1-2 asked for); (b) two listed kinds have no testable condition: 'a line will turn toward God' (what belief or doctrine measure, at what threshold) and 'a gift will come to the prophet's descendants' (does only an answer_prayer gift count, or any new trait); (c) nothing says what happens if a condition is already true when the vision is given.

**Remedy**

> For each promise kind, state the deterministic condition tested at the check; state how a hidden second clause affects keeping (required, a separate later keeping, or partial keeping with its own verse); and exclude or define promises whose condition already holds at give time.

### R2-2 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> Step 5 now commits an answer to Open Question 1 (the promise is kept by the world's course, plus a hidden second clause on some kinds), yet Open Question 1 still lists those same mechanisms as undecided alternatives ('Options: each vision has a second clause the game chooses; or a vision is fulfilled partly by the world's own course; ...') and Next Steps still says Open Question 1 blocks step 5. The reader cannot tell what is decided.

**Remedy**

> Either mark Open Question 1 as settled by step 5 and narrow it to what remains open (e.g. which kinds carry a hidden clause and how it is chosen), or move step 5's 'how it works' text back into the open question.

### R2-3 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The design does not say what happens to a prayer record once it is answered: whether it closes (freeing one of the people's 32 open slots), whether it can be answered again or in more than one form, whether a merged record's later repeats are answered too, and whether it leaves the HUD's waiting list. The cap, the HUD and the snapshot all depend on this state change.

**Remedy**

> Define the answered state: an answered record closes and moves to the remembered entries (or stays open), whether further answers are allowed, and how a new prayer of the same kind at the same place after an answer is recorded.

### R2-4 — clarity (minor)

**Problem**

## Section 4: Performance

Carried forward, not asked:

- Band work is bounded by the 64-walker cap (D10). Encounter checks look only at tiles within reach of each walking band, so a tick costs O(walking bands x reach), not O(peoples squared).
- Routes are planned once per band with A* over `culture::step_cost`; budding is capped per check, so planning cost per check is bounded. The bench gets a 64-band case.
- Standings are stored only for pairs that have met (a `BTreeMap` keyed by the pair, smaller id first), not a full matrix, so save size grows with contact rather than with peoples squared.
- Leagues are a membership list per league, read when a skirmish resolves.
- Budget stays 8 ms a tick. Measured 3.4 ms at sim-year 2,000 before bands; the sweep records tick time at each checkpoint.

## Outside voice

An independent Claude subagent reviewed Stage 1 (Codex is unavailable on this login). Eight findings; code claims checked against `settlements.rs:105`, `culture.rs:471`, `world.rs:787`. Six are mechanical and adopted into the plan; two change play and are asked as D16 and D17.

Adopted, not asked:

- **The 100-year clock** counts from a band's formation and never resets, whether the band re-plans or flees. A band whose search finds no reachable unclaimed land caches that result until the next 25-year settlement pass, so a stranded band costs nothing per tick.
- **A settlement's people** is reset to the majority people in its catchment at every 25-year pass, so after a split the daughter owns its own catchments and "never into another people's" stays true. Whether "3+ peoples with 30+ tiles" (kept under R9) still holds when spread runs through camps is measured by the sweep; if it fails, that is a design question for the author, not a quiet test change.
- **One merge for peoples:** `merge(a, b)` covers line (the larger side keeps it, as R2 says; lower id on a tie), population, standings, leagues, scripture, language, notables and each settlement's people, so "marry in" and a joined band have one rule to call.
- **Meetings:** "within reach" is a hex distance; a pair of peoples meets at most once per 10 sim-years; standings drift back toward each line's baseline when peoples stop meeting, so neighbors neither lock into feud nor kinship by repetition.
- **Raid strength** is computed fresh at the meeting from current headcounts, not the 10-tick cached aggregates.
- **Ids and tables:** standings and agreements live in a sparse `BTreeMap` keyed by the pair (lower id first), holding only pairs that have met, pruned when a people dies out; a daughter people inherits its mother's standings and agreements; the culture id is range-checked before the `u16` cast so it can never reach `NO_CULTURE`. Bands get monotonic `u32` ids, never reused and saved in the snapshot, so a queued player act cannot land on the wrong band; the bud queue rotates round-robin from the last settlement served, so old cities cannot starve new ones.

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-26

## Problem Statement
Build a god-game in which civilizations rise on a newly generated Earth-like world. Humanity starts at a single origin (Eden) and migrates outward. As peoples spread, they adapt physically to their regions and become distinct cultures. Over time they form kingdoms and invent buildings, technology, cultures, religions and languages, and they wage wars as resources and human nature collide. The player is a god with powers: shape terrain, paint biomes, unleash disasters (tornadoes, meteors, nukes), spawn creatures, and give rewards.

The player's core role, chosen in this session: **guide a favorite people.** You pick one people, then reward and nudge them from Eden onward while the rest of the world reacts.

## What Makes This Cool
- **Religion is the people's theory of you.** God-games usually treat the player like weather. Here, every blessing, meteor and spared enemy is evidence that a people uses to build doctrine about the god. Smitten rivals form their own religions about the same god. Holy wars come out of the player's own history.
- **Scripture as the history log, and it gets things wrong.** The player never reads a neutral event feed. They read holy text written by named witnesses ("In the year the sky burned, the Watcher struck the Red Valley for its pride"). When a people splits, each daughter culture's copy of the scripture drifts as its language drifts. What actually happened and what is remembered pull apart, and the player can play into that gap.
- **Visible divergence.** Peoples split at rivers and mountains. Their skin tone, build, dress, architecture, script and words drift apart, and every split traces back to choices the player made.

## Constraints
- A side project for fun (Builder mode). The goal is something delightful and playable, not a business.
- Engine: **Godot 4** (latest stable) on a Mac (Apple Silicon).
- Language: **Rust via godot-rust (gdext)** for the simulation core, and **typed GDScript** for the UI, input and glue. The user chose this in the session.
- **No C#:** C# web export in Godot 4 is unsupported or at best experimental (Godot 4.3 docs; forum reports targeting 4.7/4.8).
- **Web build is a stretch goal:** gdext's wasm support is behind the `experimental-wasm` feature flag. It needs Emscripten and export templates with GDExtension support (`dlink_enabled`). Mac is the primary target.
- New procedurally generated world every game. No real-Earth map.

## Premises (agreed)
1. The chosen people get the deep simulation. Rival civilizations run at lower fidelity, as aggregate polities.
2. **Revised after the second opinion:** a hybrid simulation.
   - **Base layer:** population cohorts on tiles, stored in packed arrays.
   - **Individuals on top**, capped at about 2,000 alive at once:
     - one tracked family line descended from Eden;
     - **witnesses** promoted out of a tile's cohort when a power touches it, with traits sampled from the tile's distributions;
     - 50 to 200 notables for the chosen people (kings, prophets, inventors, heroes);
     - 5 to 15 notables for each rival polity.
   - **Dead notables become "legends":** text records that live on in scripture and never tick again.
3. Physical adaptation comes from environment and isolation. Skin tone tracks UV (latitude). Stature and build track temperature. Lung capacity tracks altitude. Adaptations give cosmetic differences and small regional perks, and never a ranking of better or worse peoples. All peoples are fictional and are not mapped to real-world groups.
4. Religion (the belief model) is the core differentiator and is built early.
5. **First playable** (the "Eden build", Milestone 5): one generated world, Eden, migration, 3 to 5 diverging cultures, the 6 first powers, and belief, adaptation and language drift.
   - It is set entirely in the Stone Age, with no tech progression. The Stone→Bronze tech tree arrives in Milestone 7.
   - Later eras and the late-game powers come after that.
   - *(Revised after spec review so it matches the milestone order.)*
6. Distribution: itch.io **Mac build first**; attempt a web build at Milestone 5, since gdext wasm is experimental. Steam only if it proves fun. *(Revised: the user chose godot-rust.)*

## Cross-Model Perspective (Claude subagent, independent read)
- **Coolest version:** scripture as an unreliable narrator that drifts with language. Daughter cultures go to war over whose version of the meteor is true.
- **What excites the user most:** "pick one people, reward and nudge them from Eden onward". Attachment and lineage matter more than a world simulator.
- **Premise 2:** revise rather than reject. Populations alone turns "guiding a favorite" into "guiding a heatmap". Use witness promotion, a family line, and notables that become legends.
- **Performance:**
  - Typed GDScript over packed arrays: about 12 to 20k tiles in 3 to 8 ms per tick, plus about 2k individuals in 2 to 5 ms. That works out to about 50 to 100 sim-years per second on a 160×80 map.
  - C# would be 10 to 30 times faster, but it blocks the web build.
  - GDExtension (C++) is the fastest option, and it exports to the web. On the web, threads need SharedArrayBuffer, which itch.io has a toggle for.
- **The 50% head start:** port the algorithms from Azgaar's Fantasy Map Generator (MIT, JavaScript): heightmap and climate, biomes, culture expansion weighted by biome cost, and Markov-chain name generation. Azgaar produces a snapshot, so these parts are ours to build: time, migration, adaptation, god powers, belief, scripture drift, and individuals.
- Agreed with one adjustment: the family line is a thread, not a lose condition. If the family dies out, that is a story beat.

## Approaches Considered
- **A: Scripture Slice.** A throwaway weekend prototype. Rejected because most of it would be rewritten for the real game. Its scope becomes Milestones 1-2 of B instead.
- **B: Layered Sim Core, shipped in milestones.** **Chosen.**
- **C: Chronicle-First.** History runs in fast era bursts, and the player acts between eras. Rejected because it gives up the live sandbox and the spectacle of the powers that the user described.

## Recommended Approach: Layered Sim Core

### Architecture
- **The simulation is separate from what's drawn on screen.** The sim is a Rust crate (`sim/`), exposed through one gdext `Sim` class. Rendering reads the sim's state, and the sim never reads rendering state.
- **WorldState**, one Rust `Vec` per field, per tile:
  - terrain and climate: `elevation`, `moisture`, `temperature`, `biome`;
  - people: `pop`, `culture[k]` (k≈4 floats), `traits` (skin, stature, cold, altitude);
  - beliefs and identity: `belief[m]` (m≈6 axes), `language_id`, `polity_id`;
  - resources: `resource` flags.
- **Grid:**
  - an odd-r hex grid (pointy-top hexes in rows, odd rows offset half a tile east) that wraps east to west (design, Grid), with temperature driven by latitude; *(Revised 2026-10-27: user directed hex tiles — six connecting sides for territory and true circles for every radius in the game. Storage stays a flat `Vec` per field; only the neighbor math and distance metric changed.)*
  - Milestone 1 uses 160×80 (12.8k tiles);
  - the target size is 320×160 (51k tiles).
- **Fixed tick:** 1 tick = 1 sim-year in every era (D24). Speed runs from 1× to 100 years per second.
  - Systems run in a fixed order: Climate (rare), Population, Migration, Drift/Blend, Traits, Belief, Polity, Tech, War.
  - Each system is a single Rust function, `fn tick(world: &mut WorldState, rng: &mut Pcg64, dt: u32)`, so each one can be tested on its own.
- **Determinism:**
  - Every system gets its own seeded RNG, derived from the world seed.
  - A saved game is the seed, the player's action log and a periodic snapshot.
  - The same seed plus the same actions gives the same history. That makes bugs reproducible and "share your world seed" a feature.
  - The exact rules are in Simulation contracts → Determinism scope.
- **Event bus:**
  - The sim emits typed events, such as `power_used`, `famine`, `culture_split`, `notable_born`, `notable_died`, `war_declared` and `invention`.
  - The Chronicle/Scripture system and the UI subscribe to these events.
- **Individuals:**
  - a `Vec<Notable>`, capped at about 2k;
  - they tick yearly;
  - when one dies, it is demoted to a legend record (text only).
- **Rendering:**
  - The map is one hex-column mesh, not an `Image`. Rust keeps returning one RGBA pixel per tile from `Sim.paint_overlay()`; Godot lays those colors onto hex-column meshes in which every tile is a pointy-top hexagon raised as a solid column. *(Revised 2026-10-27, second pass: the first renderer drew square diamonds on the hex grid, with no sides and per-tile height jitter; the user directed hex tiles with thickness and smoothed heights.)*
    - **Heights:** a land column's top is its elevation above sea level, softened toward the mean of its six neighbors and rounded to `HEIGHT_STEPS` terraces, so neighbors rise in readable ledges instead of jitter. Water tiles sit flat at sea level.
    - **Slopes:** each top corner is shared by three tiles. Where all three are non-mountain land within `SLOPE_STEPS` terraces of one another, the corner sits at their mean height, so tops tilt into each other and the land rolls with no wall. Mountains, the sea and steeper drops keep their ledges. *(User direction 2026-10-27: "smooth toward the other land except for cliffs and mountains".)*
    - **Peaks and ridges:** a mountain's center rises into a summit (`PEAK_PX`, varied per tile and taller with altitude), and the midpoint of every edge it shares with another mountain rises by a share of the lower summit, so neighboring peaks join in ridge lines and a lone mountain stands pointed. Mountains slope only into other mountains (within `MOUNTAIN_SLOPE_STEPS`), so a range reads as one massif with cliffs where it meets the lowland. Faces above `SNOW_PX` take snow. *(User direction 2026-10-27: "design mountain peaks and ridges from angling the hex tiles".)*
    - **Thickness:** wherever a ledge remains, each column shows its two camera-facing sides (south-west and south-east) down to the top of the lower neighbor on that side, or to the floor along the map's south edge. Rows are emitted back to front, so nearer columns cover farther ones.
    - **Light** is baked per vertex on the CPU from the terraced height field: tops shade by slope toward the art bible's upper-left light, south-west sides are lit and south-east sides fall in shadow. One `ShaderMaterial` adds only the time-based and overlay terms: a slow ripple on water, a wet rim at coastlines, an antique-gold edge on the Chosen's tiles, and the night pass with the beacon. No TileMapLayer.
  - Repaint is gated on `Sim.revision()` (R6): the color texture updates only when a tick ran or an action applied; geometry rebuilds when a Raise or Lower changed heights, which is valid because powers apply only at tick boundaries (D9).
  - **Settlements are buildings, not glyphs or markers.** Each settlement is a cluster of small procedural houses drawn with polygons — a box with a lit wall, a shaded wall and a pitched roof — standing on its founding tile's top in a y-sorted node, so nearer buildings cover farther ones.
    - Tier sets the cluster: a camp is two or three tents; a village three or four houses; a town six or more houses around a tower; a city a ring wall with houses and towers inside.
    - Walls take the biome's building material (timber in forest, mud brick in desert and grassland, stone in mountains); roofs take the people's dress color; a faith mark rides the tallest roof once the people hold a doctrine; the Chosen's settlements carry a gold pennant.
    - Notables, the beacon and wonders join the same y-sorted node.
  - **Bands and wrap:** the terrain is cut into full-width bands of `BAND_ROWS` rows, one mesh each, drawn back band first so the painter's order stays exact. A power rebuilds only the bands within reach of the tiles it changed (`Sim.changed_tiles`), and the loading screen builds one band per frame. Each band, and the settlement layer, has a copy one world-width to the west and east, and the camera wraps, so the east-west seam never shows (design, Grid). Godot culls bands and copies outside the view. *(Revised 2026-10-27: one mesh rebuilt in full took 484 ms per power at 160×80; bands take about 50 ms in GDScript. Building the bands in Rust is the next step if that hitch matters.)*
  - Camera: a fixed three-quarter view. The hex plane is foreshortened vertically by `TILT` with no rotation, so hexes stay hexes and north stays up. Drag to pan, wheel to zoom, WASD/arrows. Screen→tile undoes the foreshortening, rounds to the nearest hex in cube coordinates, and checks raised columns in the rows nearer the camera first, since a tall column can cover the tile behind it.
  - Overlay modes: biome, culture, traits, belief, polity, language.
  - Scripture is an event log (verses plus kingdom events for the Chosen) in a collapsible bottom strip; one selection-driven panel floats on the map (settlement card or polity panel). *(Revised 2026-10-27: map-first UI, no permanent rail.)*

### Simulation contracts (added after spec review)
- **Cultures are discrete entities.**
  - Each `Culture` has:
    - identity: `id`, `parent_id`, `founded_tick`, `name_seed`;
    - a `centroid: [f32; k]` vector;
    - `favor: Chosen | Other`;
    - a `faith_id`;
    - a phoneme set (seeded at founding, used for names from Milestone 2 and for the full language in Milestone 5);
    - a scripture corpus;
    - aggregates refreshed every 10 ticks (population, tile count, belief).
  - Every tile stores `culture_id`. A tile switches to a neighboring culture only when its `culture[k]` vector is closer to that culture's centroid than to its own by a margin of 0.05. The margin prevents flicker.
  - **Membership:**
    - only tiles with a population of at least 1 carry a `culture_id`;
    - a tile emptied to 0 clears its `culture_id`;
    - a tile newly settled by migration takes the source tile's `culture_id` and `culture[k]`.
  - Split components are computed over member tiles only. That way empty mountains and deserts separate peoples.
- **Split rule** (checked every 25 ticks):
  - For each culture, find its connected land components (8-neighbor).
  - A component becomes a candidate daughter culture if all three hold:
    - it covers at least 30 tiles;
    - it holds at least 5% of the culture's population;
    - its population-weighted mean `culture` vector is at least 0.25 from the parent centroid.
  - A candidate must pass on 2 consecutive checks (hysteresis) before it splits.
  - The split creates a new `Culture` with `parent_id`, copies the parent's scripture and phoneme set (which then drift), and emits `culture_split`.
- **Chosen vs. rival.**
  - Eden's culture starts `Chosen`. **Exactly one culture is `Chosen` at any time.**
  - **On a split:** the daughter containing the family heir's tile stays `Chosen`. If no daughter contains the heir, because the line died out or the heir lives elsewhere, the most populous daughter stays `Chosen`. Every other daughter becomes `Other`.
  - Before polities exist (Milestones 1-5), a "rival" is simply any culture whose favor is `Other`. From Milestone 6, polities group cultures, and the rival fidelity rules apply per polity.
  - When a culture becomes `Other`, its notables over the 15-notable budget are demoted to legends, lowest salience first. Salience = scripture mentions + rank.
  - **Re-anointing:** a player action that sets another culture to `Chosen` and the previous one to `Other`.
    - It raises the `jealous` belief axis in the previously chosen culture.
    - The family line stays where it is. The player may adopt a new house in the newly chosen culture, which becomes the tracked line, while the old line is demoted to legends.
    - Its notable budget grows again from that point. Demoted notables are not restored.
- **Belief lives per tile; doctrine reads per culture.**
  - Tiles hold `belief[m]` and update from what they witness.
  - Each culture's belief is the population-weighted mean over its tiles, refreshed every 10 ticks. Doctrine thresholds and schisms read only the culture-level value.
  - **Schism (new `faith_id`):** checked every 25 ticks.
    - For each faith, compute its founding-belief vector.
    - Any culture whose belief is at least 0.4 from it (Euclidean distance over the 6 axes, each axis in [0, 1]) for 2 consecutive checks gets a new `faith_id`.
    - The new faith is founded on that culture's current belief.
    - With three or more cultures in one faith, each one is tested on its own, and cultures that diverge in the same check and are within 0.2 of each other share one new faith.
- **Witness rule.**
  - **Witness set** for a power use = the tiles in its footprint, plus tiles within a visibility radius:

    | Power | Visibility radius (tiles) |
    |---|---|
    | Meteor | 6 |
    | Tornado | 3 |
    | Raise/Lower | 2 |
    | Spawn | 2 |
    | Bless | 1 |
    | Paint | 1 |

  - **Outcome score** per tile, over the 5 ticks after the event, clamped to [−1, 1]:
    - (actual Δpopulation% − expected Δpopulation% from the tile's 10-tick pre-event trend) + Δcarrying-capacity%.
    - Scores with |outcome| < 0.05 count as "no effect".
  - **Belief update** is applied at the end of that 5-tick window:
    - a negative outcome raises `wrathful` and `capricious`;
    - a positive outcome raises `benevolent` and `protective`;
    - witnessing with no effect lowers `absent`.
  - **Promotion:** each power use promotes 1 witness (2 for Meteor and, later, Nuke). The witness comes from the most-populous affected tile, preferring a `Chosen` culture. A tile with no population can't supply a witness.
- **Individual budgets** (hard cap 2,000):

  | Category | Budget |
  |---|---|
  | Family line | ≤ 20 |
  | Chosen notables | ≤ 200 |
  | Witnesses | ≤ 300 |
  | Each `Other` culture | ≤ 15 |

  - A witness that isn't elevated to prophet, heretic or hero within 20 ticks is demoted back into its cohort, leaving a legend record if scripture cites it.
  - When the cap is reached, the lowest-salience individual becomes a legend. Witnesses go first, then `Other` notables. The family line is never evicted by the global cap.
  - **When one category is full:** a new member evicts the lowest-salience member of that same category to a legend.
    - The family line keeps the heir and the heir's nearest siblings by age, up to 20.
    - Extra siblings become ordinary cohort members, and a legend record is kept only if scripture cites them.
  - Legends are compact records (id, name seed, culture, birth tick, death tick, role, scripture refs, about 48 bytes) with no pruning. They are stored in the snapshot.
- **Family line:** one heir per generation plus their living siblings. The heir is the eldest surviving child. The line follows the heir's tile through splits. If the line dies out, that is a story beat (scripture records it), and the player may adopt a new house when re-anointing.
- **Scripture lives in Rust.**
  - Each culture's corpus is a list of `Verse { tick, template_id, slots, witness_id, mutation_state }`.
  - Rust selects templates, inherits copies at splits, applies language mutation and renders text through `Sim.scripture_text(culture_id, from, count)`.
  - GDScript only displays the text it's given. That keeps scripture in the save and in the `cargo test` determinism check.
- **Player actions are queued.**
  - Every state-changing player call (`apply_power`, `reanoint`, adopting a house) enqueues an action. Queued actions apply at the start of the next tick, before Climate, in the order they arrived.
  - The action log records `(tick, kind, params)` (powers include x, y), and replay feeds the log back at the same ticks.
- **Determinism scope.**
  - Replay is guaranteed only on the same sim build and the same platform/target.
  - The sim is single-threaded. Any future parallelism must use fixed-order chunked reductions.
  - Transcendental math goes through the `libm` crate, not platform intrinsics.
  - "Share your seed" promises the same generated world on the same build. Cross-platform world matching is best-effort.
- **Snapshot contents:**
  - `SAVE_VERSION` and `SIM_BUILD` (crate version + git hash). Mismatched saves and replays are rejected with a message.
  - Tick counter, world seed and every per-system RNG state.
  - All `WorldState` arrays.
  - Cultures with their scripture and language state.
  - Notables, family line and legends.
  - Pending queued actions, pending 5-tick outcome windows and undrained events.
  - Hysteresis state: split-candidate pass counters, schism pass counters, witness 20-tick elevation timers, and the 10-tick pre-event population trends.
- **Frame and tick scheduling.**
  - The sim runs on the main thread, time-sliced. Each frame, `step()` runs whole ticks until either the fractional-year accumulator is spent or an 8 ms budget is used. Any leftover carries to the next frame.
  - No worker thread, which keeps the web build simple.
  - 1× = 1 year per second, with speeds 1×, 5×, 25× and 100×.
  - The 100 years/s target is a benchmark gate measured in Milestone 1 (`cargo bench`), not an assumption. If it is missed, the speed ceiling drops, or a worker thread is reconsidered for Mac only.
- **Latitude correction.**
  - Tile area scales with cos(latitude). Carrying capacity and population capacity are multiplied by cos(lat). Migration cost per east-west step is multiplied by cos(lat), since tiles are narrower toward the poles. North-south cost is unchanged.
  - Rows above ±75° latitude are permanent ice with zero carrying capacity.

### Language decision: Rust sim core (godot-rust / gdext) from day one
- **Decision (user, this session):** the simulation is written in Rust from the start, not ported to it later.
- **Rust owns:**
  - `WorldState` (plain `Vec<f32>` / `Vec<u8>` per field);
  - world generation;
  - every per-tile system (Population, Migration, Drift/Blend, Traits, Belief updates, Polity);
  - individuals and notables;
  - the seeded RNG (`rand_pcg` or `rand_chacha` per system);
  - the event queue.
- **Exposed to Godot** as a single `#[derive(GodotClass)] Sim` node API:
  - `step(years)`
  - `apply_power(kind, x, y, params)`
  - `drain_events() -> Dictionary` — `{events: [{tick, kind, culture, tile, polity, text}], dropped}`, oldest first, text already in words; `dropped` is R4's count *(revised 2026-10-27: the shape built in M6a; the first kinds are tier_up, tier_down, building, kingdom, government, protest and riot)*
  - `scripture_text(culture_id, from, count) -> PackedStringArray`
  - `reanoint(culture_id)`
  - `paint_overlay(mode) -> PackedByteArray` (RGBA for the map `Image`)
  - `inspect_tile(x, y)`
  - `inspect_notable(id)`
  - `save()` / `load()`
- **GDScript owns:**
  - the camera, input and power toolbar;
  - the scripture panel (it displays text rendered by Rust and holds no state), inspectors and overlays;
  - speed controls and menus.
- **Rule:** no sim state lives in GDScript. GDScript only calls the `Sim` API and renders what it returns.
- **Why:** Rust makes the 320×160 target map and 100 or more sim-years per second cheap, and there's no port later. Rust's types also keep a large simulation's data honest.
- **Costs accepted:**
  - a Rust toolchain (`rustup`), and a `cargo build` before running in Godot;
  - the `.gdextension` file must point at the debug or release `.dylib`;
  - the web build is experimental.
- **Web stretch (Milestone 5):**
  - Target `wasm32-unknown-emscripten` with gdext's `experimental-wasm` feature, using nightly Rust if gdext still requires it.
  - Match the threads setting between gdext (`experimental-wasm-nothreads` or not) and Godot's web export.
  - Use export templates with GDExtension support. If this fails, the Mac build ships alone.
- **Testing:** sim systems are unit-tested with `cargo test`, with no Godot required. The determinism test (same seed + action log gives an identical event stream) also runs in `cargo test`.

### Game systems (target scope across milestones)
- **Powers (first playable, 6):**
  - Raise/Lower land
  - Paint biome
  - Bless (fertility or reward)
  - Meteor
  - Tornado
  - Spawn creatures (beasts that hunt, or herds to domesticate)

  Later powers: plague, volcano, gift of an invention, and nukes. The flood is no longer an ordinary power; it is the Great Flood judgment (Deep systems section 5). Nukes are available as a god power in any era. Before the atomic age, peoples read them as a sky-fire god. After it, rivals may blame each other.
- **Belief model:**
  - Each people has a belief vector over the god's observed character: wrathful, benevolent, capricious, protective, jealous, absent.
  - Every power use updates the belief of the tiles that witnessed it, weighted by its outcome for them.
  - Doctrines crystallize when a belief axis passes a threshold, and they shape behavior. A wrathful god leads to sacrifice and militarism. A protective god leads to expansion near sacred sites.
  - A schism happens when daughter cultures' belief vectors diverge.
- **Scripture:**
  - Template lines keyed to the power, the witness's culture and their belief.
  - Scripture is written in the culture's own words (from the language system).
  - Each daughter culture inherits its parent's scripture and mutates it as its language drifts.
- **Language:**
  - Each culture has a phoneme inventory and a 100-word core vocabulary.
  - Words drift at a rate scaled by isolation; contact blends vocabularies.
  - All place, person and god names come from the culture's vocabulary, and a language family tree can be viewed.
- **Settlements and kingdoms:** tiles above a density threshold become settlements. Polities form by conquest or federation. Building styles depend on biome, culture and era.
- **Tech:** inventions are discovered from pressure and resources, plus contact with other peoples.
  - The first playable (Eden build) is Stone Age only. The Stone→Bronze tree arrives in Milestone 7.
  - Later eras: Iron, Classical, Medieval, Gunpowder, Industrial and Atomic.
- **War:** driven by resource scarcity, borders and belief divergence. Battles are resolved as aggregates, between polities. Holy wars come from schisms.

## Deep systems: buildings, social balance, science, captivity and exodus, the Great Flood (added 2026-10-26)
These replace the one-line "Settlements and kingdoms" and "Tech" bullets above. All three follow the existing sim contracts:
- they live in `sim-core` with no godot dependency;
- they run on the fixed tick, with deterministic collections and seeded RNG;
- their state is in the snapshot;
- every big moment is a priority event, and scripture can record it.

The player never builds, legislates or researches directly. This stays a god game: you shape these systems through powers, covenants and prophets, and your people do the rest.

- **Doctrine proposes, covenants bind — graded by bond strength** (user decisions 2026-10-27 and 2026-10-27 later same day). A doctrine practice — a wrathful people's sacrifice, for example — is the people's own reading of the god's conduct, never a thing they do *for being Chosen*, and it exists with or without the player's approval. The god, however, may bind the Chosen directly by **decree**, without a covenant, in four kinds: ethical, lifestyle, dietary, and religious. A decree is to the Chosen only (other peoples only ever see the god's conduct), and the standing quota is **3**, after which each further capacity must be unlocked by a stronger covenant bond — each covenant tier raises the quota by 3, a broken covenant resets it. A decree does three things: it vetoes a doctrine practice inside its kind (a no-sacrifice decree silences the wrathful altar), it writes behavior (a dietary law reshapes food wants from Milestone 6, an ethical law moves legitimacy and unrest), and it taxes belief if it fights what the people saw with their own eyes — the more a decree contradicts the doctrine their history wrote, the more tension it raises, until defiance shows as transgression (D13's lanes) and can seed schism or Reformation. Your conduct writes their doctrine; your decree and your covenant bound their law.

### Shared design rules
- **People decide, the god nudges.** Each system has a "need" driver that the sim computes and a "values" driver that comes from culture and doctrine. The player's levers act on those drivers, never on a build menu.
- **Faith is visible.** Each system has at least one place where doctrine changes what you see on the map or read in scripture.
- **Faith drives progress** (the user's principle, 2026-10-26; printing is one example of it). Devotion is a source of advancement, not a brake on it. It works through:
  - **scripture pressure,** which leads to Writing, the Printing press and literacy;
  - **benevolent doctrine,** which leads to sanctuary-hospices and Medicine;
  - **temples and scriptoria,** which grow into Knowledge buildings such as universities;
  - **just doctrine and kept covenants,** which lead to law, Liberty and consent-based government;
  - **Reformation,** which renews faith and lowers inequality.

  Faith only erodes where the people's picture of the god is capricious or wrathful. So the god's own conduct, not learning itself, decides whether a people grows in faith as it grows in knowledge.
- **Slow meters, fast events.** Meters refresh on the existing 25-tick check, with hysteresis. Events (a riot, an invention, a finished wonder) fire immediately and go through the event lanes.
- **Data, not code, for content.** The building catalog and the invention graph are data files in `sim-core/data/` (`buildings.ron`, `inventions.ron`). They are loaded at worldgen and hashed into `SIM_BUILD`, and SAVE_VERSION is bumped when their schema changes. Content tuning never needs a code change.

### 1. Buildings and settlement advancement (Milestone 6 → 9)

**Settlement tiers.** A tile becomes a settlement once its population crosses a threshold. Higher tiers spread over neighboring tiles.

| Tier | Name | Population | Footprint | Building slots | Earliest era |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Camp | 50+ | 1 tile | 1 | Stone |
| 1 | Village | 200+ | 1 tile | 3 | Stone |
| 2 | Town | 2,000+ | 1 tile | 6 | Bronze |
| 3 | City | 10,000+ | up to 3 tiles | 10 | Iron |
| 4 | Great city | 50,000+ | up to 7 tiles | 14 | Classical |
| 5 | Metropolis | 250,000+ | up to 13 tiles | 20 | Industrial |

- **Demotion:** a tier drops only after population stays below 80% of its threshold for 2 checks.
- **Ruins:** a destroyed or abandoned settlement leaves a ruin on the tile. A ruin keeps its name, and scripture can mention it.

**Building lines.** Each line upgrades in place as eras and inventions unlock it. One slot holds one building at its current level.

| Line | Levels (era-gated) | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Food | granary → irrigation works → terraces → mills → mechanized farms | **Granary:** absorbs one famine, refills over 10 years. **Irrigation and later levels:** +10–25% carrying capacity on the settlement's tiles; irrigation needs a river or coast. |
| Defense | palisade → stone walls → citadel → bastion fort → bunker | Lowers the chance of being conquered and the losses when raided. Walls also shield buildings from tornado damage at levels 2+. |
| Faith | shrine → temple → great temple → cathedral/sanctuary | **Piety** +. A temple raises scripture fidelity: verses drift 30% slower per level, since priests copy them. The form follows doctrine (see below). |
| Rule | council fire → hall → palace → capitol | **Legitimacy** +. The capital's rule building sets the government's seat; destroying it causes a legitimacy crash. |
| Knowledge | elder circle → scriptorium → academy → university → laboratory | **Research points** +. A scriptorium also keeps written scripture (after Writing), which slows drift further. |
| Trade | barter ground → market → harbor → guildhall → exchange | Trade links to settlements within range, +prosperity, and +invention diffusion along the links. A harbor needs a coast. |
| Craft | workshop → foundry → manufactory → factory | Turns resources into goods (+prosperity). Needs the matching invention, such as Bronze working for a foundry. |

**Construction.**
- Each settlement earns **labor** each tick: population × (1 − subsistence share).
- The subsistence share falls as food buildings and inventions improve.
- Labor fills a single build slot at a time.
- **Choosing what to build** (once per 25-tick check): each candidate's score = its *need* × its *culture weight* × its *doctrine modifier*.
  - **Need examples:** a food deficit makes Food urgent; an enemy polity nearby makes Defense urgent; low legitimacy makes Rule urgent; research pressure makes Knowledge urgent.
  - **Culture weight:** each culture has a 5-value profile (martial, devout, mercantile, scholarly, communal), drifting like the rest of its culture vector.
  - The highest score wins.
  - Ties break by the lowest catalog index, so construction stays deterministic.
- **Upkeep:** each building level costs labor upkeep. If a settlement can't pay, its buildings decay one level per 50 sim-years.

**Faith made visible: doctrine shapes buildings.**

| Dominant doctrine | Faith building becomes | Side effect |
|---|---|---|
| Wrathful | Altar-fortress: walls plus a sacrificial altar | +Defense; a small population cost per decade (sacrifice) |
| Benevolent | Sanctuary-hospice | Faster recovery from famine and plague |
| Protective | Sacred-site shrine, built on the tile where you last blessed them | Pilgrimage: migration pull toward the site |
| Capricious | Oracle house | +Research toward astronomy and omens |
| Just (from covenants) | Hall of law | +Legitimacy; transgression episodes close 25% faster |
| Absent | Shrine falls to ruin, and a civic or knowledge building takes the slot | Secular drift (see Science) |

**Wonders.**
- **Limit:** at most 1 wonder per culture per era.
- **Trigger:** a large labor surplus for 50+ sim-years, plus a dominant value: devout leads to a monument to the god; scholarly to a great library; martial to a great wall; mercantile to a great harbor.
- **A wonder to you is an act of devotion, and your answer matters.**
  - Bless it: piety rises sharply, and scripture records your approval.
  - Smite it: the classic "tower struck down" story. It raises `just` if the people are under judgment and `capricious` if not.
  - Ignore it: `absent` rises a little.
- Every wonder is a named landmark with a verse.

**On the map.**
- Each settlement shows as a composite glyph:
  - roof and wall shape from the culture's style seed;
  - material color from the biome (timber, mudbrick, stone, ice-block);
  - tower count by tier;
  - a faith mark by doctrine.
- In the Kinetic Sand Glyphs direction, settlements read as raised marks in the relief. Wonders are gold-edged when they're dedicated to you.
- The inspector's settlement card shows:
  - the tier;
  - building slots as a glyph ring, matching the D6/D7 motif;
  - the current build and its progress;
  - the strata bar (section 2).

**Player levers.**
- **Bless a settlement:** +50% labor for 10 years.
- **Meteor or tornado:** destroys buildings; walls reduce tornado damage.
- **Raise or Lower land:** can strand a harbor or flood irrigation.
- **Gift of invention** (later power): unlocks a building level early, recorded as a revelation.

### 2. Social balance (Milestone 6 basic → Milestone 8 full)

**Strata.** Every settlement tracks 4 strata:
- commoners;
- artisans and merchants;
- priesthood;
- rulers and warriors.

Each stratum stores its share of the population and its share of the wealth. Together with the settlement's buildings, these roll up to the polity level each check.

**The six meters** (per polity, 0–1, refreshed each 25-tick check with hysteresis):

| Meter | Driven by |
|---|---|
| **Prosperity** | Food plus goods per person, trade links, recent disasters (−) |
| **Inequality** | Wealth concentration across strata (a Gini-style measure). Rises with conquest, tribute and trade wealth; falls with Food buildings, benevolent doctrine and revolutions. |
| **Legitimacy** | The ruler's acceptance. Its source depends on the government: piety for a theocracy, the dynasty notable's renown for a monarchy, prosperity for a republic. |
| **Piety** | Strength of culture-level belief, plus faith buildings, minus the `absent` axis |
| **Liberty** (custom) | Rises with Reformation, kept covenants against oppression, and `just`/`benevolent` doctrine; falls under autocracy and wrathful doctrine. Leads to Republic and Democracy. |
| **Cohesion** | How alike the polity's tiles are culturally and in faith. Falls when one polity spans several cultures or faiths. |
| **Unrest** | Rises with inequality, low prosperity, low legitimacy and low cohesion. Adds +0.15 while the culture is under covenant judgment (D13) and +0.1 for 10 years after a disaster. |

**Governments.** Governments evolve; nobody picks them.

```
Band ─► Chiefdom ─┬─► Theocracy ──┬─► Holy empire
                  │               └─► (revolution) ─► Republic
                  ├─► Monarchy ───┬─► Empire ─► Constitutional monarchy (Industrial)
                  │               └─► (revolution) ─► Republic ─► Democracy (Industrial)
                  └─► Merchant league (needs Trade lvl 3, mercantile value) ─► Republic
                  any ─(coup/collapse)─► Autocracy (Gunpowder+)
```

- **How a government changes:** threshold rules, checked each 25 ticks. A condition must hold for 2 checks.
  - Theocracy forms when piety is above 0.7 and the priesthood holds 30% or more of the wealth.
  - Republic forms when Liberty is above 0.6, merchants hold 30% or more of the wealth, and the polity knows Philosophy. Liberty mostly comes from faith (Reformation, covenant-keeping, just doctrine; see Science).
- **What a government changes:** which strata hold power, the legitimacy source, and the culture's build weights. For example, a theocracy raises the weight of Faith buildings.

**What unrest does.** Each outcome is an event, and most write a verse.

| Unrest | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 0.5+ | **Protest.** A verse is written, and the build queue favors Rule or Food. |
| 0.65+ | **Riot.** −2% population in the largest settlement, and one building damaged. |
| 0.75+ for 2 checks | **Rebellion.** The polity splits along its least-cohesive border. The new polity is led by an `Other`-budget notable. If the cultures also differ, it feeds a culture split. |
| 0.75+ with low legitimacy | **Revolution.** The government changes to the most fitting successor. The ruler notable falls and becomes a legend. |
| Priesthood unrest with the faith already diverging | **Schism.** Brings the existing schism check forward to this check. |
| High unrest plus low prosperity | **Exodus.** A migration wave. It can breach the land covenant, so the people sin out of desperation, which the player sees and must judge. |

**Notables in society.** Rulers, high priests and merchant princes are notables, using the existing budgets. Their *ambition* trait drives:
- coups (rulers);
- heresies (priests);
- secession (merchants).

The family line from Eden can rise into rule or fall into the commoner stratum. The inspector shows where they stand.

**Player levers.**
- **Bless:** prosperity.
- **Meteor on a palace:** legitimacy crash, which risks a revolution.
- **Bless the poor quarter:** lowers inequality.
- **Prophets:** a covenant prophet adds piety.
- **Social covenants at Milestone 8** (detectable now that the meters exist):
  - "Do not oppress the poor": breached when inequality stays above 0.6 for 2 checks.
  - "Honor the ruler I raise": breached by a revolution or coup against a ruler you blessed.

**Safety and tone.** Labor customs are abstracted as "free / tribute / bonded" on the commoner stratum and affect inequality and unrest. The game never models real-world peoples or institutions. All peoples stay procedurally fictional (premise 3).

### 3. Science and invention (Milestone 7 → 9)

**The invention graph.**
- About 64 inventions across 8 eras, in `inventions.ron`.
- Each invention has:
  - prerequisites (a DAG);
  - one or more *pressure* tags (food, war, disease, trade, sky, travel, record, **scripture**);
  - optional resource or biome requirements, such as copper or a coast;
  - effects: building unlocks, capacity or combat modifiers, and belief hooks.
- An era is reached when a culture knows 60% or more of that era's core inventions.

| Era | Core inventions (abridged) |
|---|---|
| Stone | Fire mastery, Knapping, Foraging lore, Shelter, Burial rites, Spoken law |
| Bronze | Agriculture, Animal husbandry, Pottery, Weaving, Bronze working, **Writing**, Wheel, Calendar |
| Iron | Iron working, Masonry, Irrigation engineering, Coinage, Sailing, Road building |
| Classical | Philosophy, Mathematics, Medicine, Engineering, Astronomy, Codified law |
| Medieval | Mills, Heavy plough, Printing precursor (block print), Optics, Universities |
| Gunpowder | Gunpowder, Navigation, **Printing press** (strongly pulled by scripture pressure), Banking, Anatomy, Telescope |
| Industrial | Steam power, Railways, Chemistry, Germ theory, Telegraph, Electricity |
| Atomic | Flight, Radio, Antibiotics, Rocketry, Computing, **Nuclear fission** |

**How discoveries happen** (each 25-tick check, per culture):
- **Research points:** RP = √population × (1 + 0.25 × knowledge building levels) × (1 − unrest) × contact bonus × doctrine modifier.
- The culture's RP accumulates toward the eligible invention whose pressure tags best match its current needs:
  - famine pushes food inventions;
  - nearby war pushes war inventions;
  - plague pushes disease inventions;
  - many meteors and omens push sky inventions.
- When the accumulated points reach the invention's cost, it is discovered. That emits an `invention` event, writes a verse, and promotes an inventor notable.

**Diffusion.**
- Each check, every known invention may spread to neighboring cultures and to trade partners.
- The chance scales with contact (shared borders, trade links) and language similarity (cultures in the same language family learn faster).
- Daughter cultures inherit everything their parent knew.

**Lost knowledge.**
- An invention known by only one settlement, or only one culture, can be lost if that settlement is destroyed or falls to tier 0. A meteor on the only academy city can start a dark age.
- Lost inventions stay in scripture as legends ("the old ones could raise water without hands") and are cheaper to rediscover.

**Science and faith: where the game's theme pays off.**
- **Writing:** before it, scripture is oral and drifts at the base rate. After it, written scripture drifts 50% slower; scriptoria and temples slow it further. Daughter cultures that diverged before Writing keep their differences.
- **Doctrine changes the research mix:**
  - capricious: +sky;
  - wrathful: +war;
  - benevolent: +disease and medicine;
  - just: +record and law;
  - absent: no bonus.
  - devout cultures with written scripture: +record. The wish to read and copy scripture is a research pressure of its own (see Printing and Reformation below).
- **Printing and Reformation: faith drives freedom** (the user's direction, 2026-10-26).
  - **Scripture pressure:** a culture with Writing, high piety and many temples or scriptoria builds up a *scripture* research pressure, the wish to copy and spread the holy text. It is the strongest pull toward Block print and the **Printing press**. So devout peoples tend to invent printing first.
  - **The printing press:** scripture drift drops a further 50%. Literacy spreads, raising research points and cohesion. Printed verses also spread the faith along trade links.
  - **Reformation:** literate believers read scripture for themselves and compare it with how the priesthood and rulers behave. This fires when a culture has the Printing press, piety is above 0.6, and either the priesthood holds 35% or more of the wealth or a ruler notable breaks a covenant. It is a **renewal**, not a decay:
    - priesthood wealth is redistributed, and inequality falls;
    - a *Liberty* custom is adopted, raising legitimacy-by-consent;
    - the faith may split into a reform branch and a traditional branch, using the existing schism mechanics with the reform branch keeping higher piety;
    - and a verse proclaims it.
  - **Freedom grows from faith:** Liberty is a polity custom, 0–1. It rises through Reformation, through the covenant "Do not oppress the poor" being kept, and through `just` and `benevolent` doctrine. It falls under autocracy and under wrathful doctrine. High Liberty is the main path to Republic and later Democracy (see Governments).
- **Discovery and faith depend on the god your people believe in.** Some inventions *re-interpret your powers*, and how a culture takes this depends on its doctrine:
  - In a culture whose god is *just* or *protective* (a god of order and law), a discovery reads as **revealing the Watcher's design**. Piety rises, and the verse says so ("the heavens keep the Watcher's law").
  - In a culture whose god is *capricious* or *wrathful* (a god of whims and terror), the same discovery reads as **explaining the god away**:
  - **Astronomy:** future meteors are witnessed as "falling stars" more often than as divine wrath; the witness's belief update is ×0.6.
  - **Germ theory:** later plague powers stop reading as punishment.
  - **Telescope** and **Chemistry:** similar effects.
  - In those capricious or wrathful cultures, each such discovery raises `absent` a little and can trigger a **heresy event**, in which a priest notable rejects the new knowledge. That raises schism risk inside the faith.
  - The late-game tension follows from this. A people who learned to see you as a just lawgiver grow in faith as they learn. A people who knew you only through terror and whim may stop believing you exist. **How you behaved for 5,000 years decides whether science deepens your people's faith or dissolves it.**
- **Nuclear fission:** your people can build nukes too. Your nuke power then gets blamed on rival nations (as already planned), and wars can go nuclear on their own. Scripture of the atomic age records both.

**Player levers.**
- **Gift of invention** (later power): grant one eligible invention through a prophet. It's recorded as revelation, raises piety, and can't be "explained away".
- **Protect or destroy knowledge cities:** Bless or Meteor the academy city.
- **Covenant at Milestone 8:** "Seek knowledge" or "Do not question the heavens". The latter is breached when an explaining-away invention is adopted, which forces a real choice between faith and progress.

**UI.**
- A per-culture **knowledge web** (Tab from the inspector): a radial glyph-ring graph in the Kinetic Sand direction, with known, eligible, lost and never-seen states.
- **Era advance** is a great event: a short ceremony card naming the era in the culture's own words.

### 4. Captivity and exodus (Milestone 8, built on war and conquest)
Peoples can conquer and carry off other peoples. The god can then set captives free and lead them to a land of their own. Captivity is shown in the abstract: bonded labor, lost freedom of movement, and slow loss of identity. There are no depictions of cruelty. All peoples stay procedurally fictional (premise 3).

**How captivity happens.**
- **Who can be taken:** when a polity wins a war or raid, it may take part of the losing population as **captives**. Any culture can capture any other, including your Chosen people, and your Chosen can be captors themselves.
- **How much:** up to 15% of the defeated tiles' population.
- **Likelihood:** higher for martial-value cultures and wrathful doctrine. Lower when the captor's Liberty is high, or when it has a kept covenant "Do not hold captives" (Milestone 8 law).
- **Where they go:** captives are moved to the captor's settlements, as a **captive cohort** on the host tile.

**Life in captivity** (each 25-tick check):

| Effect | Rule |
|---|---|
| Labor | Captives add labor to the captor's settlement, and the captor's inequality rises |
| No movement | A captive cohort can't migrate; it grows slowly (×0.5 growth) |
| Assimilation | The captive culture vector drifts toward the captor's by 0.02 per check, slowed by captive piety (up to ×0.25 at high piety). When it gets within 0.1 of the captor's centroid, the cohort merges and that people's identity is lost. |
| Faith | Captive piety rises at first ("by the rivers we remembered"). Captives write scripture about the captivity, and their language persists as long as piety holds. |
| Cry | Captive unrest builds up as **outcry**. High outcry promotes a captive witness to **prophet of deliverance** (it needs notable budget; captive cohorts count against the captive culture's budget). |

The god hears the cry. When outcry passes 0.6, a priority `outcry` event fires: "The <people> cry out in bondage under the <captors>." The captive cohort gets a pulsing gold-ochre mark on the map. If the captives are your Chosen, this always fires.

**The deliverance arc** (player-led, about 50–150 sim-years):

```
 OUTCRY ──► PROPHET RAISED ──► DEMAND ("Let my people go")
                                   │
                    captor resolve vs. dread
                       │                 │
                   refuses            releases ──────────────┐
                       │                                     │
         SIGNS (your powers on the captor):                  │
         swarms/beasts (Spawn), tornado, meteor,             │
         later plague and darkness; each raises dread        │
                       │                                     ▼
                 dread > resolve ─────────────────►  EXODUS BAND forms
                                                         │
                       PURSUIT? (captor army follows) ◄───┤
                                                         ▼
               GUIDE: you place a beacon, the band follows it
               SEA/RIVER: Lower land to open a path; Raise behind to close it
               WILDERNESS: the band crosses barren land, needs Bless (food)
                                                         ▼
               PROMISED LAND: the band settles where you mark it
               ─► new settlement, land covenant offered, freed people's verse
```

- **Dread and resolve** (both per captor polity, 0–1).
  - Resolve is the captor's will to hold on: captive labor share × legitimacy × (wrathful or martial weight). It rises a little each time it refuses.
  - Dread rises with each sign aimed at the captor's settlements. It gets a bonus when the sign follows a demand within 10 sim-years, so it reads as an answer to the prophet.
  - The captor releases when dread is greater than resolve. Its scripture records the signs as the wrath of the captives' god.
- **The exodus band** is a new kind of sim entity: a **migrating band**.
  - It holds a population, a culture, a faith, a notable leader (the prophet) and a position.
  - It moves up to 1 tile per sim-year toward the player's **beacon**, a pillar marker you can move at any time (cloud by day, fire by night).
  - It eats the food capacity of the tiles it crosses. On barren tiles it loses 2% population per year unless you Bless it ("bread from heaven").
  - It can't cross water, unless you **Lower land** to open a path. Raising land behind it closes the path on pursuers.
  - It disbands if it reaches 0 people or if the beacon goes untouched for 100 sim-years (it settles where it stands).
- **Pursuit:** if the captor's resolve was high when it released, there is a chance it changes its mind and sends a force after the band. That uses the war system from Milestone 8.
  - **Fight:** the band fights as an aggregate battle against the pursuing force.
  - **Escape:** the band escapes once it crosses water or a closed path.
  - **Turned back:** a turned-back force raises the captor's dread permanently.
- **The promised land:** when the band reaches a tile you have marked as its destination (a right-click on the beacon), it founds a tier-1 settlement.
  - The freed people regain a normal tile cohort and can migrate again.
  - You're immediately offered the **land covenant** (D18), with the promised-land region seeded around the arrival tile.

**What deliverance means to each people.**

| People | Belief change | Scripture |
|---|---|---|
| The freed people | +benevolent, +protective, and +just if the captors had sinned (or held your Chosen); piety surges | A founding narrative: their exodus verse becomes the opening of their scripture, and later copies drift from it |
| The captors | +wrathful (signs), +dread doctrine | "The year the swarms came" and the loss of their servants |
| Onlookers | Witnesses within the radius of signs and the band's path update their belief as usual | Their own versions of the story |

- **Freeing a non-Chosen people** makes them a natural candidate for anointing. The re-anoint card notes "They owe you their freedom." Re-anointing still makes your previous people jealous (D11), unless your previous people were the captors under judgment (D13).
- **Your own people as captors.** If your Chosen hold captives, the cry comes from inside your own house. Freeing them means sending signs against your own people:
  - it raises `just` in the freed people, and judgment pressure in your own;
  - your own scripture may record it as betrayal, or as repentance, depending on their piety.

  A covenant "Do not hold captives" makes this a sin you can judge.
- **Doing nothing.** Unanswered captives keep assimilating until their identity is gone. Their last verse is a lament, and they become a legend in the language tree.

**UI.**
- **On the map:** captive cohorts show as a dotted inlay of the captive culture's color inside the captor's territory. The hover readout says "<people>, in bondage to <captors>".
- **Inspector:** for a captive cohort it shows outcry, the prophet, the captor's dread versus resolve, and **[Hear their cry]**. That button raises the prophet and starts the demand.
- **During the exodus:** the band is a cluster of glyphs with a fading trail. The beacon is a gold pillar marker you drag. A wilderness readout shows food left and years wandering.
- **Ceremonies:** release, the sea crossing (if you open one) and arrival in the promised land are all great events with a ceremony card, as in D5.

**Architecture.**
- **New sim-core module:** `captivity.rs`, which holds the captive cohorts, outcry, dread and resolve, and the migrating bands.
- **Captive cohorts:** stored as a `BTreeMap<TileId, Vec<CaptiveCohort>>` side table (deterministic), capped at 2,000 cohorts. Each cohort holds the captive culture id, population, culture vector, belief and captor polity.
- **Bands:** a `Vec<Band>`, capped at 16. A band moves along a deterministic path (A* over the tile grid with fixed tie-breaks) toward the beacon each tick.
- **New player actions:** `hear_cry(cohort)`, `place_beacon(band, x, y)`, and `mark_destination(band, x, y)`. They go through the same action log as powers (R3, D9), so replay stays deterministic.
- **New events:**
  - `outcry`, `release`, `exodus_begin`, `crossing` and `arrival` (priority lane);
  - `pursuit` and `band_hardship` (normal lane).
- **Snapshot additions:** captive cohorts, bands, beacons, dread and resolve, and outcry.

**Tests.**
- Capture moves at most 15% of the population into captive cohorts.
- Assimilation merges a cohort once it's within 0.1, and high piety slows it ×0.25.
- Outcry over 0.6 promotes a prophet.
- Release happens exactly when dread passes resolve.
- A band never crosses water without a lowered path.
- A band on barren land loses 2% per year unless blessed.
- Arrival founds a settlement and offers the land covenant.
- Pursuit is stopped by a closed path.
- A full exodus replays deterministically under the run-twice test.

### 5. The Great Flood (the god's greatest judgment, Milestone 8)
The god can cleanse the earth with water from the depths. A select few are saved in a vessel built at the god's command, and the world starts over from them. This is the most powerful act in the game, and the hardest to take back.

**Availability.**
- **When:** the flood unlocks once the world has 3 or more cultures and 1,000 or more sim-years of history.
- **Who decides:** it is always the player's choice. The game never floods on its own.
- **How often:** once per world, unless the god declines to make the **Promise** afterward (see below).
- **Safety:** choosing it forces an autosave and needs a 3-second hold to confirm.

**The arc.**

```
 1. CHOOSE THE FEW      pick a household: a notable with piety ≥ 0.7, plus their
                        kin (up to 8 named people + their cohort, ≤ 60 people)
          │
 2. THE WARNING         their prophet receives the command; a verse is written;
                        neighbors who hear it update belief (most mock: +absent;
                        a few may join, if their piety is high and space remains)
          │
 3. BUILD THE VESSEL    a special build on the household's tile, paid in labor
                        and timber from forest tiles within 3; takes ~60–120 sim-
                        years depending on size; size sets capacity
          │
 4. GATHER THE KINDS    choose which creature kinds and plant stocks go aboard;
                        capacity is limited, so not everything can be saved
          │
 5. THE DEEP RISES      fountains of the deep + rain: sea level climbs over
                        ~40 shown days (one sim step, animated); every tile below
                        the flood line is submerged; all life there is lost
          │
 6. THE RIDING          the vessel drifts; the world is water; the map shows only
                        the vessel and the highest peaks (if any stand above)
          │
 7. THE WATERS RECEDE   ~150 shown days; erosion reshapes the land: valleys
                        deepen, plains gain fertile sediment, coastlines move
          │
 8. THE LANDING         the vessel rests on the nearest high ground; the few step
                        out onto a new world: a second Eden
          │
 9. THE PROMISE         the god may bind itself never to flood again (a sign set
                        in the sky); the choice shapes every people after
```

**Choosing the few.**
- **Who qualifies:** any notable with piety of 0.7 or more, of any people.
  - It's usually your Chosen family line (the heir's household), so the line from Eden survives.
  - Choosing a household from another people is allowed. It works as a re-anointing (D11), so the flood becomes a new covenant with a new people.
- **The household:** up to 8 named individuals plus their tile cohort, capped at 60 people.
- **Joining:** during the warning, a neighbor with piety of 0.8 or more may join the household while space remains (event: "and some believed").

**The vessel.**
- **What it is:** a special one-time building, the **Vessel**, on the household's tile.
- **Cost:** labor from the household, plus timber drawn from forest tiles within 3 tiles.
- **Size:**

| Size | Build time | Capacity (people / kinds) |
|---|---|---|
| Small | ~60 sim-years | 20 people / 8 kinds |
| Great | ~90 sim-years | 40 people / 16 kinds |
| Vast | ~120 sim-years | 60 people / 24 kinds |

  If there isn't enough timber nearby, the build stalls. You can Paint forest nearby to provide it.
- **Watching it:** neighbors watch it rise.
  - Scripture records the mockery and the faithfulness.
  - Each decade of building is a chance for more neighbors to join or to scorn.
- **If you strike early:** if you start the flood before the vessel is finished, the vessel is lost. No one is saved except people on the highest peaks (see flood depth). The game warns you plainly.
- **If the household dies first** (by war, plague or your own meteor): the vessel is abandoned. You must choose again.

**The kinds.**
- **What gets saved:** you choose which creature kinds go aboard. These are the ones from Spawn creatures: grazers, hunters, birds, beasts of burden. You also choose plant stocks: grain, forest, orchard.
- **What's lost:**
  - A creature kind left behind is extinct in the new world, and Spawn can never summon it again.
  - A plant stock left behind regrows only where wild seed survived on peaks; it never comes back as a crop.
- **The cost of a gap:** not saving grain means the new people must re-domesticate it (the Agriculture invention restarts).

**Flood depth** (chosen when you begin the flood):

| Depth | Flood line | What survives |
|---|---|---|
| **The Deep** (default) | Above all land except the highest 1% of tiles | Only the vessel, plus scattered peak survivors (usually none) |
| **The Great Water** | Above all land except the highest 10% | The vessel, plus small remnant peoples on high mountains, who keep their own flood memory and scripture |

- Remnant peoples are a deliberate choice. Their scripture tells the flood from their side, and their language stays a separate branch, so the world has more than one account of the same judgment.

**What the few carry into the new world.**
- **Culture:** their culture vector, language and phoneme set.
- **Scripture:** the household's full copy. The flood verse becomes the new opening, the "first book" of the new age.
- **Knowledge:** only the inventions the household knew. Everything else is **lost knowledge**: legends with a lower rediscovery cost. Cities, wonders and ruins of the old world appear on the map as **drowned ruins** that later peoples can find. They trigger a verse ("the cities beneath the sea") and can be dug up in later eras, when Archaeology (Industrial) gives back some lost inventions.
- **Traits:** carried over. Adaptation then starts again from the household's traits.
- **The family line:** continues if the household was the line. Otherwise the household's head founds a new line.

**Belief after the flood** (for survivors and remnants):

| World state before the flood | Survivors' belief | Remnants' belief |
|---|---|---|
| Most peoples under judgment (D13) or holding captives | +just, +protective (they were spared for righteousness) | +just, +wrathful |
| Few peoples under judgment | +wrathful, +capricious, mixed with gratitude | +wrathful, +capricious; their scripture names the god cruel |

The game shows this before you confirm: "The world is mostly righteous. Your people will remember this as cruelty."

**The Promise** (after landing; a one-time choice):
- **Make the Promise:**
  - The flood power is removed for the rest of this world.
  - A sign appears in the sky after every storm from then on.
  - The new people gain a high `just` and `protective` belief, and their scripture centers on the promise. Every later people that inherits the scripture inherits the promise.
  - If you ever unleash a disaster that kills more than 30% of a people afterward, they may write that you broke faith (+capricious).
- **Withhold the Promise:**
  - The flood can happen again after 2,000 more sim-years.
  - The new people live in dread: +wrathful, and doctrines of appeasement form.
  - Their scripture tells of waters that may return.

**On the map and in the UI.**
- **Rising water:** the flood plays as a ~40-second sequence at any game speed. The sea rises tile by tile from the low ground; settlements go dark, then sink.
- **Scripture during the flood:** the scripture panel shows only the household's verses.
- **Riding out the flood:** the map is the Kinetic Sand palette washed to deep umber water. The vessel is a gold glyph drifting slowly over it.
- **Receding:** water drains over ~60 seconds, revealing reshaped land. Drowned ruins show as dim glyph marks.
- **The vessel's build:** shown as a settlement building with a progress ring in the inspector. The household card shows capacity used: people and kinds.
- **The Promise:** a ceremony card ("Set your sign in the sky?") with [Make the Promise] and [Withhold].

**Architecture.**
- **New sim-core module:** `flood.rs`, handling the household, vessel, kinds, flood phases, erosion pass, landing, drowned ruins, and the promise flag.
- **Flood phases:** the whole flood resolves in **one sim tick**, so determinism and the tick model are unchanged:
  1. raise the sea level;
  2. clear all life below the flood line (populations, settlements and notables become legends; buildings become drowned ruins);
  3. run an erosion pass over the elevation field (a fixed number of deterministic diffusion iterations);
  4. lower the sea level;
  5. place the vessel on the nearest high tile and found the new cohort.

  The ~40 s and ~60 s sequences are **pure GDScript playback** of the before and after elevation and water masks returned by the Sim. They hold no sim state, keeping R5/R6 rules.
- **Extinction:** a creature kind or plant stock left behind is removed from the spawnable set and the crop list.
- **New player actions:** `choose_household`, `set_vessel_size`, `choose_kinds`, `begin_flood(depth)` and `make_promise(bool)`. They go through the action log, so replay is deterministic.
- **New priority events:** `warning`, `vessel_complete`, `flood_begins`, `landing`, `promise`.

## Covenants: laws, sin and judgment (D12, user answer 2026-10-26; resolves Open Question 2)
- **Giving a law:**
  - You may give the Chosen people up to 3 covenants, chosen from behaviors the sim can detect.
  - A covenant is delivered through a promoted witness who becomes a prophet. It enters scripture as a verse in the people's own words.
  - Like any verse, it drifts with language and is copied at splits, so daughter peoples can come to disagree about what was commanded.
- **Laws available by milestone:**
  - **Milestone 3:** "Do not strip the land" (a tile's carrying capacity falls from overuse), "Keep apart from strangers" or its opposite, "Welcome the stranger" (culture blending with `Other` peoples), and "Do not leave the land I gave you" (migration beyond a sacred region).
  - **Milestone 6:** settlement laws.
  - **Milestone 8:** "Do not make war on your kin" (war against a culture with a shared ancestor) and "Spare the conquered".
- **Sin:**
  - A violation emits a priority-lane `transgression` event. It never gets dropped, and it pins in the scripture panel.
  - The offending tiles get a dark-ochre mark for a few seconds.
  - The inspector shows a transgressions list for the culture.
  - If the culture's piety is high, a confession verse is written.
- **Judgment:**
  - A new belief axis, `just`, joins the existing 6, making 7 axes. The schism distance becomes Euclidean over 7 axes; the 0.4 threshold is to be re-tuned.
  - Harm to transgressing tiles within about 10 sim-years of a sin raises `just`. Harm with no recent sin raises `wrathful` and `capricious`, as before.
  - A Bless on transgressors (mercy) raises `benevolent` and weakens that covenant's hold.
  - Ignoring repeated sins lowers the covenant's hold until it fades from practice, though not from scripture.
- **UI:**
  - A "Covenants" tab in the scripture column header lists each law as the people remember it, next to what you actually gave. That makes the drift visible.
  - New covenants are chosen from a list with plain-language costs.
- **Losing the anointing (D13, user answer 2026-10-26):**
  - A Chosen culture with 3 or more unrepented transgressions within 100 sim-years is **under judgment**. This shows in the inspector and the Covenants tab.
  - Judgment unlocks "Withdraw your favor" on the re-anoint card. The player always decides; withdrawal is never automatic.
  - Withdrawal after judgment raises `just` and a guilt doctrine in the old people, instead of `jealous`. It also writes a verse of rejection and keeps one "faithful remnant" notable who remembers the covenant.
  - Without judgment, re-anointing behaves as in D11 (jealous).
- **Engineering follow-up needed:** detection functions per law, covenant state in the snapshot, the `transgression` event type, the 7th belief axis, and tests for each. This was not covered by the eng review, so rerun /plan-eng-review before Milestone 3.

- **Motion and the knowledge web (DR2-D8):**
  - **Motion budget:** at most 2 pulsing marks on screen. Priority: Tier A arc, then outcry, then beacon, then verse edge, then nudges. Everything else renders steady.
  - **Reduced motion:** pulses become steady gold outlines.
  - **Knowledge web:**
    - concentric rings by era: the current and next era drawn in full, past eras collapsed into a labeled summary ring;
    - node states: known (solid), eligible (outline), lost (cracked);
    - hover shows the culture's script plus a translation;
    - **L** switches to a plain sortable list view.
- **Small windows (DR2-D9):**
  - The inspector popover is at most 360×440 px at 100% UI scale. It scrolls inside, flips sides to stay on screen, and never covers the tile it inspects.
  - Below 1200 px, the flood panel and the Polity and Covenants tabs open as a 440 px right-side sheet.
  - **Keys:** Ctrl+1/2/3 switch popover tabs; Alt+number picks an arc chip; Enter and Backspace step through the flood panel. Esc closes or cancels whatever is on top: aiming first, then sheets.
  - Every chip and tab has a screen-reader label.
- **Speed rules (DR2-D10):**
  - Effective speed = min(player speed, every active arc cap).
  - The caps apply regardless of the pause setting.
  - Choice pauses always happen.
  - The chip of the cap currently in force is highlighted.
- **Wonder reply (DR2-D11):**
  - A completed wonder is a Tier A choice: [Bless] or [Leave it be].
  - Dismissing the card keeps a 30-year chip.
  - **Smiting** means actually casting Meteor or Tornado on the wonder tile within the window. The aiming preview reads "Strike the <wonder>: their people will call it just / capricious".
  - When the window expires, the wonder counts as ignored (a small +absent).
- **Flood playback (DR2-D12):**
  - During playback, map input and powers are disabled; camera pan and zoom still work.
  - The household's verses play as subtitles.
  - Holding Esc for 1 s skips to the landing. This is allowed only after the profile has watched one full flood, and always in replays. The skip state lives in user settings, not the sim.
- **Era card language:** follows D6. It shows a line in the culture's script plus a translation, for the culture whose era it is. No new decision was needed.
